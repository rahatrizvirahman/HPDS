# Project Rules

## Editorial style — no AI-pattern dashes or mid-sentence colons

Em dashes used for stylistic separation and mid-sentence colons used to hinge a claim to its elaboration are markers of AI-generated writing. This rule applies to all prose: paper sections, plan documents, chat responses, and comments.

**Em dashes for stylistic separation:** Rewrite the sentence. Use commas, semicolons, or separate sentences. Do not mechanically swap the dash for another punctuation mark.

**Mid-sentence colons:** Never introduce an explanation, elaboration, or result with a bare `:` mid-sentence. The pattern to avoid is `[general claim]: [specific elaboration]`. Instead:
- Split into two sentences.
- Use a subordinating conjunction ("with", "where", "meaning", "suggesting") to attach the elaboration.
- Use parentheses for brief asides.

**Do not modify dashes that are part of:**
- Compound words or hyphenated adjectives
- Technical terminology (e.g., MoE--TCN, teacher--student)
- Names, products, or brands
- Code, file paths, or command-line flags
- Mathematical notation or numeric ranges (e.g., 0.039--0.060, P2--P3)
- Quoted text

The final output should read as if edited by a human, not mechanically transformed.
